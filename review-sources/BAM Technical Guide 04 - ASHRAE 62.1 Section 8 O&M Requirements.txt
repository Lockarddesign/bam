ASHRAE 62.1 Section 8: The Operations and
Maintenance Requirements Most Buildings Are
Failing (and What Changed in 2025)
BAM Technical Team | August 2026 | buildingandair.com/blog
NADCA Certified | ASHRAE Member | OSHA-30 Certified | EPA Certified | PA COSTARS Vendor



     Everyone reads Section 6 for the ventilation rates. Almost nobody reads Section 8, which tells
     you what has to happen to filters, coils, drain pans, dampers, and outdoor air after the building is
     occupied. ASHRAE rewrote the whole table in 2025. Here is what it now requires.


Ask a facility director what ASHRAE 62.1 requires and you will hear about outdoor air rates: cfm per
person, cfm per square foot, Table 6-1. That is Section 6, and it governs design. The section that
governs the building you are running today is Section 8, Operations and Maintenance, and in most
buildings we walk into, nobody has read it.

That matters more now than it did a year ago. ASHRAE published Standard 62.1-2025 in late 2025, and
on September 30, 2025 approved Addendum h to 62.1-2022, which replaced the entire Section 8
maintenance table with a new one built around inspection, verification, and validation. If your preventive
maintenance program was written to an older edition, or was never written to any edition, this is what the
current standard expects.


Does Section 8 Apply to Your Building?
Section 8.1.1 of the 2022 edition says the section applies to buildings, ventilation systems, and
components constructed or renovated after the adoption date of the section by your jurisdiction.
Addendum h rewrites that to systems "placed into service," which drops the constructed-or-renovated
limiter; once a jurisdiction adopts that text, Section 8 reads as applying to existing buildings in service.

Adoption runs through the mechanical code. The 2024 International Mechanical Code references
ASHRAE 62.1-2022. The 2021 IMC, which Pennsylvania's Uniform Construction Code adopted effective
January 1, 2026, references 62.1-2019. New Jersey, Maryland, and New York adopt an IMC edition
statewide; Delaware adopts by county. In practice, a building permitted in the last decade in our territory
was designed to somewhere between 62.1-2007 (Pennsylvania stayed on the 2009 codes until late
2018) and 62.1-2019, and the Section 8 tables in those editions are close cousins of the 2022 version.

If your building predates adoption, Table 8-1 is not enforceable against you today. It is still the benchmark
that a commissioning agent, an insurer's engineer, an IAQ consultant investigating a complaint, or an
expert witness will measure your program against. For healthcare facilities, ASHRAE 170 governs the
clinical spaces, but 62.1 governs everything else on the campus and is referenced by 170 for outdoor air
on mixed systems.


What Section 8 Requires Beyond the Table
Four requirements sit in the text and are missed as often as the table itself.

An O&M manual (Section 8.2). Written or electronic, kept on site or in a centrally accessible location for
the working life of the equipment, updated as the system changes. It must contain the O&M procedures,
ventilation system operating schedules and any changes to them, final design drawings, maintenance
schedules based on manufacturer instructions, and the Table 8-1 inspection and maintenance
requirements. Addendum h adds the maintenance tasks from ASHRAE/ACCA Standard 180.

Reevaluation on change of use (Section 8.1.2). When occupancy category, occupant density, or use
changes in a way that no longer matches the design assumptions, the ventilation design, operation, and
maintenance must be reevaluated and the manual updated. A conference room converted to an open
office, or a storage room converted to a fitness space, triggers this.

Operation consistent with the manual (Section 8.3). Systems must be operated so that spaces receive
Section 6 ventilation during periods of expected occupancy. An outdoor air damper closed for energy
savings during occupied hours is a Section 8.3 violation.

The turnover package (Section 7.2.6). At construction close-out, the owner must receive and retain the
O&M manual, control drawings and sequences, the air balance report, record drawings, and the design
criteria. If you cannot find your balance report, this is the section that says you should have one.

Two items stand out for anyone who manages coils. The standard expects coils to be checked and

What Addendum h and the 2025 Edition Changed
Addendum h, approved by ASHRAE and ANSI on September 30, 2025, replaced Table 8-1 with a table
titled Minimum Inspection, Verification, and Validation Activity and Frequency for Ventilation Systems and
Associated Components. The committee's reasoning was that the old table duplicated maintenance tasks
from ASHRAE/ACCA Standard 180, so the new table is built on inspection rather than maintenance, and
Standard 180-2018 becomes a normative reference for the maintenance itself.

The new table distinguishes two kinds of tasks. "Verify" tasks (suffix a) can be done by visual inspection,
documentation review, gauges, or BAS trend data without special tools. "Validate" tasks (suffix b) require
calibrated instruments, adjustments, and specialized personnel. If a task cannot be verified, it must be
validated regardless of the listed frequency.

The direction is unmistakable. The standard moved from "check the filter gauge quarterly" to "prove,
annually, that the building is getting its design outdoor air and holding its design pressure relationships,
and re-measure everything every three years." Addendum h is a published addendum to 62.1-2022
available free from ASHRAE; confirm whether your printing of the 2025 edition carries the revised table,
and either way, the committee has told you where it is headed.


The Section 5 Requirements That Make Maintenance Possible
Several Section 5 design requirements exist specifically so that Section 8 can be carried out. When they
are missing, maintenance gets skipped because it is physically impossible.

      Access (Section 5.19). Access doors or panels are required at the outdoor air intake plenum, mixed-
      air plenum, upstream face of every coil of four rows or fewer, both faces of every coil over four
      rows, air cleaners, drain pans and seals, fans, and humidifiers. A coil with no downstream access
      door will not be inspected on the downstream face, which is where the fouling is.
      Drain pans (Section 5.7). Sloped at least 0.125 inch per foot toward the outlet, outlet at the lowest
      point, P-trap or equivalent seal on draw-through units, pan extending downstream far enough to
      catch carryover.
      Filtration upstream of wetted surfaces (Section 5.5). MERV 8 minimum upstream of every cooling
      coil or other wetted device, a requirement that rose from MERV 6 in the 2013 edition. A coil running
      without a prefilter for a season will not come back with a quarterly filter change.
      Humidity (Section 5.12). Indoor dew point limited to 60°F whenever outdoor dew point is above 60°F,
      occupied and unoccupied.
      Building exfiltration (Section 5.17). In mechanically cooled buildings, total outdoor air intake must
      equal or exceed total exhaust under all load and dynamic reset conditions. A building running
      negative pulls humid air through the envelope and grows mold in the walls.
      Intake separation (Table 5-1). Cooling tower exhaust at least 25 feet from an outdoor air intake,
      cooling tower intake or basin 15 feet, loading docks and bus idling 25 feet, dumpsters 15 feet,
      plumbing vents 3 to 10 feet depending on height. Addendum h now requires an annual check that
      nothing has been built or moved inside those distances.
      Legionella (Section 5.20). The owner must receive written documentation of the HVAC water system
      design elements required by ASHRAE 188.

Frequently Asked Questions
What does ASHRAE 62.1 Section 8 require?
An operations and maintenance manual, operation of the ventilation system consistent with that manual
during occupied hours, reevaluation whenever the building's use changes, and the inspection and
maintenance activities and frequencies in Table 8-1.

How often does ASHRAE 62.1 require coils to be cleaned?
The 2022 edition's Table 8-1 requires coils to be checked semiannually and cleaned, restored, or
replaced as needed, with an annual check of drain pans, drain lines, and coils for biological growth.
Addendum h moves the maintenance task to ASHRAE/ACCA Standard 180 and requires a semiannual
inspection of the drain pan assembly.

How often must outdoor air be verified?
The 2022 table required verification of total outdoor air per air handler every five years within a 10
percent tolerance. Addendum h (2025) requires annual verification and a full test-and-balance validation
of every unit every three years, with no tolerance clause and no small-unit exemption.

Is ASHRAE 62.1-2025 adopted in Pennsylvania?
No. Pennsylvania's Uniform Construction Code adopted the 2021 International Codes effective January
1, 2026, and the 2021 IMC references ASHRAE 62.1-2019. The 2024 IMC references 62.1-2022. No
published model code references the 2025 edition yet.

Does 62.1 Section 8 apply to hospitals?
