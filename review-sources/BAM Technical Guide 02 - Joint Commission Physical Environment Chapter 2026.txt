The Joint Commission's New Physical
Environment Chapter: Where EC.02.05.01 EP 15
Went and What HVAC Surveyors Still Check in
2026
BAM Technical Team | August 2026 | buildingandair.com/blog
NADCA Certified | ASHRAE Member | OSHA-30 Certified | EPA Certified | PA COSTARS Vendor



     Effective January 1, 2026, the Environment of Care and Life Safety chapters are gone from the
     hospital manual. Fewer elements of performance does not mean fewer findings. Here is the
     HVAC crosswalk, the survey data behind it, and the documentation to have ready.


If your survey readiness binder still has tabs labeled EC.02.05.01 and LS.02.01.35, it is organized around
a manual that no longer exists. Effective January 1, 2026, The Joint Commission replaced the
Environment of Care (EC) and Life Safety (LS) chapters of the hospital and critical access hospital
manuals with a single Physical Environment (PE) chapter, and then revised the two elements of
performance that matter most for utilities (PE.04.01.01 EPs 1 and 2, along with PE.04.01.03 EP 3) again
on March 1, 2026.

The headline number is that 48 percent of hospital EPs were eliminated. The number that matters for
your ventilation systems is zero, because none of the underlying code requirements changed. This
article walks through where the HVAC, pressure relationship, humidity, damper, and water management
requirements moved, what surveyors scored in 2025, and what to have ready when the survey team
schedules your OR suite walk for the morning of day one.


What Changed on January 1, 2026
The Joint Commission rebuilt its hospital requirements to line up with the structure of the CMS Conditions
of Participation. The EC and LS chapters, which together carried 44 standards and roughly 460
elements of performance, were replaced by eight PE standards:

Requirements that went beyond CMS regulation moved to a new National Performance Goals (NPG)

now live at NPG.11.03.01.

Two things did not change. The Ambulatory Health Care manual still uses the EC and LS chapters for
now, so an ambulatory surgery center is still surveyed against EC.02.05.01 (The Joint Commission has
signaled that the ambulatory manual may follow later). And the codes behind every deleted EP (NFPA
99-2012, NFPA 101-2012, NFPA 80 and 105-2010, and ASHRAE 170-2008 by reference) are still the
codes.

EP 15 Is Gone as an EP. The Requirement Is Not.
This is the point facility directors most need to hear from their compliance consultants and are not
always hearing.

The 2026 Survey Process Guide's Physical Environment Document List and Review Tool keeps the EP
15 language almost word for word: in critical care areas designed to control airborne contaminants, the
ventilation system provides appropriate pressure relationships, air-exchange rates, filtration efficiencies,
temperature, and humidity, with the form and frequency of assessment per hospital policy. It adds that
the basis for design compliance is the FGI Guidelines edition used at the time of design. The finding is
written at PE.04.01.01 EP 1 or PE.03.01.01 EP 3.

The Joint Commission's current FAQ says the same thing from the other direction: NFPA 99-2012
Chapter 9 requires ventilation, temperature, and relative humidity to comply with ASHRAE 170-2008 for
new, renovated, altered, or modernized areas, and existing spaces may remain as originally designed if
the system has not been altered and is maintained to the original design parameters.

The consequence structure is also unchanged. The underlying Conditions of Participation are §482.41
(physical environment) and §482.42 (infection prevention). Under the old chapter, The Joint
Commission's physical environment guidance described a ventilation finding in a critical area as a
condition-level deficiency that triggered a Medicare deficiency follow-up survey within 45 days,
reducible to a standard-level finding only if the organization repaired the process while surveyors were
on site. The CoPs behind that have not changed.


What Surveyors Found in 2025
The Joint Commission stopped publishing percentage-based "most challenging standards" lists after the
2018 data. The last count-based data, from 1,330 hospital surveys in 2023, put EC.02.05.01 EP 15 fourth
among all scored EPs in the higher-risk categories, with findings on 226 surveys, roughly one hospital in
six.

For 2025, the January 2026 issue of Perspectives replaced counts with a ranked analysis weighted by
frequency and severity. The top physical environment findings in hospitals were:

 1. Maintaining interior spaces that are safe and suitable.
 2. Ensuring critical pressure relationships.
 3. Providing a clean, odor-free environment.
 4. Properly handling and storing hazardous chemicals.
 5. Maintaining furnishings and equipment.

Under pressure relationships, surveyors reported three recurring themes: incorrect pressure in operating
rooms, sterile storage, and decontamination areas (negative where positive is required, or the reverse);
temperature and humidity in ORs and sterile areas outside range (below 68°F or above 60 percent RH)
with no corrective action or documentation explaining the deviation, found through both real-time checks
and retrospective log review; and air exchange rates below minimum, with documentation missing or
outdated and corrective actions not evident.

Read that list again. Every item is a maintenance outcome, not a design outcome. Those ORs were
balanced correctly once.

Where Restoration Fits
Most of the 2025 pressure relationship and air change findings trace to the same mechanical causes:
cooling coils fouled to the point of restricting airflow, final filters loaded past their pressure limit, exhaust
ductwork in isolation areas that has never been cleaned, and building envelope leaks from cabling and
renovation work. None of them require capital replacement. All of them require someone to measure,
correct, and document.

Frequently Asked Questions
Did The Joint Commission eliminate the ventilation requirements for operating rooms and isolation
rooms?
No. It eliminated the element of performance that restated them. The requirements come from NFPA 99-
2012, which references ASHRAE 170-2008, and they are now scored under PE.04.01.01 EP 1 with the
same language carried in the Survey Process Guide.

Which ASHRAE 170 edition does a surveyor hold my hospital to?
For spaces constructed or with plans approved on or after July 5, 2016, ASHRAE 170-2008 as
referenced by NFPA 99-2012, or the state design requirement if it is more stringent. For older spaces, the
design parameters of the edition in effect when the system was installed, provided the system has not
been altered.

Are fire and smoke dampers still tested every six years?
Yes. The specific EP was deleted, but NFPA 80-2010 and NFPA 105-2010 remain in force through NFPA
101-2012 and NFPA 90A-2012, and PE.04.01.01 EP 2 requires the documentation. See our fire and
smoke damper inspection guide.

Does my ambulatory surgery center follow the new PE chapter?
Not yet. The 2026 Ambulatory Health Care manual retains the EC and LS chapters, including
EC.02.05.01 EP 7 for airborne contaminant control and EC.02.03.05 with a four-year damper cycle.
Expect the ambulatory manual to be restructured later.

Do I still need to keep temperature and humidity logs?
The Joint Commission has no prescriptive daily logging requirement, but it expects critical rooms to be in
constant compliance when in use and a reliable strategy to prove it. Non-continuous readings must be
logged, and every non-compliant condition must be documented with corrective action. CMS interpretive
guidance at A-0726 states that hospitals must maintain records demonstrating required temperature,
humidity, and airflow levels.
